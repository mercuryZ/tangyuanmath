Lesson 1 · 15 min Stage 1 · The big idea

What is multiplication?

Before any memorizing, your child needs to see the one idea underneath it all: multiplication is fast adding of equal groups.

In this lesson your child will connect "3 groups of 4 crackers" to 4 + 4 + 4, and then to the shorter way of writing it: 3 × 4. That's the whole lesson — and it's the foundation for everything else.

Talk it through

Grab about 20 small things — crackers, coins, LEGO bricks. Real objects your child can move are worth ten explanations. Then follow the steps below; say the quoted lines out loud, they're written for you.

Do

Put 12 crackers on the table in a pile. Ask your child to share them onto 3 plates, the same number on each plate. (4 on each.)

Ask

"How many plates do we have? How many crackers on each plate?" — You want the answer 3 plates, 4 crackers each. Those two numbers are about to become a multiplication.

Say

"How many crackers altogether? You could count them one by one… or you could add plate by plate: 4, then +4 more, then +4 more. That's 4 + 4 + 4 = 12."

Say

"Writing 4 + 4 + 4 every time gets tiring. So mathematicians made a shortcut. 3 groups of 4 is written 3 × 4. The first number counts the groups. The second number is what's in each group. Same thing, shorter writing."

Do

Write 3 × 4 = 12 next to 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 on paper, and draw a little arrow between them. Have your child read it out loud as "three groups of four equals twelve."

Try it together

Let your child drive the demo below: every tap of the button adds one more plate of 4 tangyuan. Watch the adding sentence grow — then see it turn into a multiplication. Ask before each tap: "Now how many groups? What will the addition look like?"

Group machine — tap to add plates

Quick check

Two minutes, no pressure. Wrong taps just get a gentle wiggle — your child can try again as many times as they like.

Common mix-ups

  • "3 × 4 is 3 + 4 = 7." The number-one beginner mistake. × means "groups of", not "and then add". Gently re-run the plates: 3 plates, 4 each, count them all.
  • Reading × as "times" with no picture behind it. "Three times four" is fine — but always finish with "…that's 3 groups of 4" until the picture comes automatically.
  • Groups must be equal. 4 + 4 + 4 is 3 × 4, but 4 + 4 + 5 isn't any multiplication — the groups aren't the same size. Multiplication is for equal groups only.

Play it now

Finish with a winning round: ten little questions, both numbers from 1 to 5 — exactly the size of fact your child just learned to picture. Around 8 out of 10 correct means this lesson has landed.

Practice facts up to 5 × 5
Parent tip: let your child do the moving and the talking. If they say "3 groups of 4" out loud, in their own voice, with their own hands on the crackers — that sentence is worth more than any worksheet. Speed can wait weeks; the picture comes first.
