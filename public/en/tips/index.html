Tangyuan's Math Tips

Six mental-math tricks for two-digit multiplication. Every trick has conditions — when they don't match, fall back to splitting the number.

1. Split one number (the basic move)

Break any two-digit number into “tens + ones”, multiply step by step, then add. This is the foundation — every trick below is a special case of it.

24 × 13 = 24 × 10 + 24 × 3 = 240 + 72 = 312

2. Same tens, units add to 10

When the tens digits match and the units add up to 10: multiply tens × (tens + 1) for the front, units × units for the back. Pad the back with a 0 if it's a single digit.

23 × 27 → front: 2 × 3 = 6, back: 3 × 7 = 21 → 621
45 × 45 → front: 4 × 5 = 20, back: 5 × 5 = 25 → 2025

3. Same units, tens add to 10

When the units match and the tens add up to 10: the front is tens × tens + units, the back is units × units (pad to two digits).

32 × 72 → front: 3 × 7 + 2 = 23, back: 2 × 2 = 04 → 2304
41 × 61 → front: 4 × 6 + 1 = 25, back: 1 × 1 = 01 → 2501

4. Near 100? Use a base number

Write numbers as “(round ± difference)” and expand. See 25, look for 4; see 5, look for 2.

98 × 96 = (100−2)(100−6) = 10000 − 800 + 12 = 9408
25 × 24 = 25 × 4 × 6 = 100 × 6 = 600

5. Multiplying by 11 or 99

× 11: pull the two ends apart, add them for the middle (carry if needed). × 99: multiply by 100, then subtract the number itself.

34 × 11 → 3 (3+4) 4 = 374
57 × 11 → 5 (12) 7 → carry → 627
23 × 99 = 2300 − 23 = 2277

6. Cross multiplication (for anything else)

The mental version of long multiplication: units × units for the ones digit, cross-sum for the tens, tens × tens for the top — watch the carries.

34 × 56:
units 4×6 = 24 → write 4 carry 2
cross 3×6 + 4×5 = 38, +2 = 40 → write 0 carry 4
tens 3×5 = 15, +4 = 19 → 1904
