Printable Multiplication Charts (1–12 & 1–100)

Print one for the kitchen wall or the homework folder — it answers "what's 7 × 8?" in two seconds while the facts are still settling in. How to read a multiplication chart

Pick one factor from the top row and the other from the left column. Slide along the row and down the column; the cell where they meet is the product. To find 7 × 8: run the 7-row across until it crosses the 8-column — 56.

Two things worth pointing out to your child. First, the chart is symmetrical: 3 × 4 and 4 × 3 land on the same number, mirrored across the diagonal — that's the swap rule, and it means your child only has to learn half the chart. Second, any row is a skip-counting sequence: read the 3-row aloud (3, 6, 9, 12…) and you're counting by threes. That makes the chart a bridge to the skip-counting lesson in our free starter course.

Which version should I print?

The color chart is the easiest on the eyes — shaded headers keep the rows and columns from smearing together, and the squares are lightly tinted. The black & white chart is for school printers, photocopiers and strict "no color" homework rules; the grid stays crisp in pure black. The ink-saving chart drops all shading and draws the grid with thin gray lines — it's the one to pick if the chart gets reprinted every month, or if you're printing a class set of thirty.

From chart to memory

A chart is a reference, not a memory — the goal is for your child to need it less each week. Work one table at a time instead of staring at the whole grid: we have a dedicated page for each, with the patterns of that table spelled out and a practice round preset to it. Start with the friendliest ones — the 3 times table, 4 and 6 — then take on the famous troublemakers, the 7 times table and 8. The 12 times table is a great stretch goal once the rest are solid.

When pencil practice is the next step, the printable worksheets cover the same facts with an answer page you can print or skip. And when your child is ready to answer out loud, the practice game is untimed and free.

FAQ

Are these multiplication charts really free?

Yes. All six PDFs — the 1–12 chart and the 1–100 chart, each in color, black & white and ink-saving versions — are free to download and print, with no account and no ads.

What paper size are the charts?

The PDFs are laid out for US Letter paper: the 1–12 chart in landscape and the 1–100 chart in portrait. Both also print fine on A4 if you choose "Fit to page" in the print dialog.

How do I read a multiplication chart?

Pick one number from the top row and another from the left column. Run the row of the first number across and the column of the second number down — the cell where they meet is the product. Because 3 × 4 = 4 × 3, every answer appears twice, mirrored across the shaded diagonal.

Chart or practice — which does my child need?

Both, in that order. The chart is the reference your child checks while learning; the game is where the facts stick. Every chart on this page links to a matching untimed practice round, and each times table has its own practice page.
