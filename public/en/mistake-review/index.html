The mistake book: why wrong answers are worth repeating

Most practice tools throw wrong answers away. Ours collects them. This page explains how mistake review works in Tangyuan Math, why re-meeting a fact after a few days helps it stick, and how to fit it into a week. Trying it needs no account — the whole thing is free.

How it works

  • Any question answered wrong in Practice or the Challenge quietly enters the mistake book — nothing to file, nothing to copy.
  • A fact that keeps going wrong earns a small counter: it needs to be answered right more than once before it leaves.
  • One clean run and the fact is crossed out. Clear the book, and there is genuinely nothing left to review.

The result is a list that is always exactly the length of what your child does not know yet — never longer, never shorter.

Why "again, later" works

Meeting the same fact a few days apart does more for memory than meeting it ten times in one evening. Rest leaves the answer slightly faded, and pulling it back out is what strengthens it. The mistake book arranges exactly those meetings — no flashcards to organize, no schedule to keep.

We promise no more than that: a sensible way to revisit what went wrong. No app can honestly promise grades.

A simple weekly rhythm

Most days, a normal practice round. Once or twice a week, swap it for Practice My Mistakes (it appears on the game's home screen once the book has something in it). Short rounds — five to ten questions — keep the review light. Our 15-minute coaching plan shows how this fits into a daily rhythm.

Try it now

Play a round, answer a few wrong on purpose if you like, then open Practice My Mistakes — the wrong ones will be waiting. Everything stays in your own browser unless you choose to sync with a free account.
