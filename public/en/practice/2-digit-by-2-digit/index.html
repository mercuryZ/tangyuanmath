2-Digit × 2-Digit Practice

The biggest numbers in the game: questions like 47 × 63, done fully in the head. This is the mode where multiplication stops being a table lookup and becomes thinking — splitting numbers, spotting patterns, stacking partial products. Rounds of 5, 10 or 20 questions, untimed by default, so the working memory spends its effort on the math rather than a countdown.

Hints that teach six real shortcuts

Miss a question and the hint doesn't just say "wrong" — it names the fastest path for that pair of numbers and walks it step by step. The game carries six mental-math shortcuts: splitting by place value, the tens-match trick (23 × 27 below), ×11 patterns, near-tens adjustment, ×5 doubling, and the squares. The Math Tips page explains each one with worked examples, and the hints quote them mid-round, exactly when they're useful.

A question with its hint

Wrong answers still go to the mistake book, so the question that stumped you today quietly returns in a later round — with the hint available again, and again, until it's yours.

Where this mode fits

It's the summit of the practice ladder: times tables first (1–10 and 1–12), then the bridge mode (2-digit × 1-digit), then this. If the full method — not just the shortcuts — needs teaching first, the two-digit multiplication guide covers the split-apart algorithm with worked examples a parent can read along.

Prefer paper? The printable worksheets generate the same two-digit × two-digit questions as PDFs (a Supporter feature), with an optional answer page.

When untimed gets easy

Two-digit × two-digit rounds finishing comfortably? The 10-level Challenge wraps the same skills in a gentle 120-second-per-level format, starting from a level-0 warm-up — the right dose of pressure, applied after accuracy is already there.
