2-Digit × 1-Digit Practice

The first step past the times tables: questions like 34 × 6, where a two-digit number meets a single digit. It looks like "big kid" multiplication, but the move inside is one your child already owns — split the big number into tens and ones, multiply each part, add. This mode is untimed: rounds of 5, 10 or 20 questions, answered at whatever pace feels comfortable.

Start 2-digit × 1-digit practice Not yet — back to times tables

The move inside every question

Take 34 × 6. Split 34 into 30 and 4; multiply each part by 6 (30 × 6 = 180, 4 × 6 = 24 — both times-table facts); add the pieces: 180 + 24 = 204. Every question in this mode works the same way, which is exactly why it's the right bridge: it rehearses the tables and teaches the split-apart habit that two-digit × two-digit will need next.

When an answer comes in wrong, the hint walks the split out loud — tens first, then ones, then the sum — so a miss turns into a worked example instead of a red X.

A question from this round

Is my child ready?

Good fit if the 1–10 tables are mostly automatic — answered in a beat, not reconstructed on fingers. If mixed times-table rounds still need slow counting, spend a little longer there first: times-tables practice is the same game with an easier range, and the starter course covers the understanding side.

Want the reasoning first instead of the range? The two-digit multiplication guide teaches the split-apart method with worked examples, and Math Tips collects the six mental-math shortcuts used in the hints.

After this mode

When 2-digit × 1-digit rounds come back mostly clean, the natural next steps are 2-digit × 2-digit practice — where the six mental-math tricks start paying off — or the 10-level Challenge if a gentle clock sounds fun. For pencil practice, the printable worksheets include a 2-digit × 1-digit sample with an answer page.
