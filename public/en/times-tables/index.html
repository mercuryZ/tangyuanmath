Times Tables, One Page Each

Every table below gets its own page: the full fact list from 1 × n to 12 × n, the patterns of that specific table spelled out (the fours are not the sevens, and pretending otherwise is why table practice fails), three FAQ, and a practice round preset to exactly that table. No sign-up, no timers.

If the whole concept is still new, the free Multiplication Starter Course builds understanding first — these pages are the fluency step that comes after.

Pick a table

3 The 3 times tableWhere the real tables begin — count by threes, then check any answer with the digit trick. 4 The 4 times tableThe kindest middle table: double your twos, twice. 6 The 6 times tableFive groups plus one more — and two free error-checks. 7 The 7 times tableThe famous hard one. It is half the size it looks. 8 The 8 times tableDouble, double, double — the most mechanical table there is. 12 The 12 times tableThe dozen table: tens plus twos, all the way to 12 × 12.

More tables (2, 5, 9, 11) are covered by the free wins lesson, the nines lesson and the full 1–12 chart — each will get its own page too.

How to use these pages

Work one table at a time, in roughly the order above: the 3s, 4s and 6s are the friendliest, the 7s and 8s are the famous challenge, and the 12s are the stretch goal. A table page takes about ten minutes: read the patterns, say the full list aloud once, then run the preset practice round. Two or three short rounds across a few days beats one long drill — and because the game is untimed, a nervous child is never racing a clock while the facts are still settling.

Alongside the tables, the printable multiplication charts (1–12 and 1–100) make a wall reference your child can check between rounds, and the printable worksheets add pencil practice with an optional answer page.
