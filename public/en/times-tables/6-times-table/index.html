The 6 Times Table

The 6 times table sits in the middle of the chart and inherits from both neighbors: it is the fives plus one more group, and it is the threes doubled. Children who already own the 5s and 3s are most of the way to the sixes before they start.

The patterns in the 6s

Five groups plus one more

Six is five plus one, so 6 × n is (5 × n) + n. 6 × 8? Five eights are 40, plus one more 8 — 48. This is the workhorse trick for the table because most children learn the fives early (they end in 5 or 0) and adding one more group is easy mental work.

Or: the threes, doubled

Because 6 = 2 × 3, every 6-answer is the matching 3-answer doubled: 3 × 7 = 21, and 6 × 7 = 42. Two roads to the same fact is a luxury — when one slips, the other is there.

Every answer passes two checks

All multiples of 6 are even (6 is even), and all of them are also multiples of 3 — the digit sum comes out to 3, 6 or 9 (42 → 4 + 2 = 6; 54 → 5 + 4 = 9). An answer that is odd, or whose digits do not sum to 3, 6 or 9, is not in the sixes. Two free error-catchers in one table.

Watch out for 6 × 7

Ask any teacher: 6 × 7 = 42 is the most-fumbled fact in the whole chart. It is fumbled precisely because it belongs to two famous "hard" tables at once. Give it extra reps — it is the same fact as 7 × 6, and our 7 times table page has its own angle on it.

6 times table FAQ

What is the trick for the 6 times table?

Five groups plus one more: 6 × n = (5 × n) + n. For 6 × 9, take five nines (45) and add one more 9 — 54. If the fives are shaky, double the threes instead: 6 × 9 = 2 × 27.

How do I know if a number is in the 6 times table?

It must pass both checks: the number is even, and its digits add up to 3, 6 or 9. 48 passes (even; 4 + 8 = 12 → 1 + 2 = 3), 50 fails (even, but 5 + 0 = 5), 45 fails (digit sum works, but it is odd).

Why is 6 × 7 = 42 so easy to forget?

It sits in the overlap of the two tables children find hardest, so it gets less natural rehearsal than its neighbors. Extra repetition helps — and remembering that 42 = 7 × 6 too means a miss in one table can be rescued from the other.
