The 4 Times Table

The 4 times table is the kindest of the "middle" tables, because it borrows a table your child already knows: the twos. Four is two twos, so any 4-fact is just two doublings away — no new sequence to memorize, just a move to apply.

The 4 times table, 1 × 4 to 12 × 4

1 × 4 = 47 × 4 = 28
2 × 4 = 88 × 4 = 32
3 × 4 = 129 × 4 = 36
4 × 4 = 1610 × 4 = 40
5 × 4 = 2011 × 4 = 44
6 × 4 = 2412 × 4 = 48

The patterns in the 4s

Double, double

4 × n means double n, then double it again. 4 × 7: double 7 is 14, double 14 is 28. Done. Every fact in the table is reachable this way from something small, which is why the fours are a confidence table — children who freeze on "4 × 8" rarely freeze on "double 8, then double it again".

Every answer is even — and the endings cycle

Because 4 is even, every multiple of 4 is even: no odd answers anywhere in the table. The final digits walk through the same five steps forever — 4, 8, 2, 6, 0, then repeat (4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40…). If an answer ends in an odd digit, it cannot be a 4-fact.

The fours are the twos, shifted

Compare the two tables side by side: 2 × 3 = 6 and 4 × 3 = 12 — every 4-answer is the matching 2-answer doubled. This is worth saying out loud with your child once; it makes the whole table feel less like new territory and more like a known landscape seen from higher up.

Fours in real life

Four wheels on a car, four legs on a chair, four quarters in a dollar, 24 hours in a day (4 × 6) — the fours are everywhere once you start looking. Real-world hooks like "a day is 4 × 6 hours" turn abstract products into facts with a use.

4 times table FAQ

Is there a trick for the 4 times table?

Yes: double the number, then double it again. 4 × 9 = double 9 (18), double 18 (36). It works for every fact in the table and needs nothing but the twos.

Why do all the answers in the 4 times table end in an even digit?

Because 4 itself is even: any whole number times an even number comes out even. The endings actually cycle 4, 8, 2, 6, 0 — so a "4-times-table answer" ending in 3, 5, 7, 9 or 1 is definitely wrong.

How is the 4 times table connected to the 2 times table?

Every 4-fact is the matching 2-fact doubled: 2 × 6 = 12, and 4 × 6 = 24. That is the double-double trick viewed from the other side — knowing your twos means the fours are only two small steps away.
