The 3 Times Table

The 3 times table is where the "real" tables begin — after the free wins of ×1, ×2 and ×10, the threes are the first table your child has to genuinely build. The good news: it has a rhythm you can hear, and a check-any-answer trick you can do on your fingers.

The 3 times table, 1 × 3 to 12 × 3

1 × 3 = 37 × 3 = 21
2 × 3 = 68 × 3 = 24
3 × 3 = 99 × 3 = 27
4 × 3 = 1210 × 3 = 30
5 × 3 = 1511 × 3 = 33
6 × 3 = 1812 × 3 = 36

The patterns in the 3s

Count by threes, out loud

The 3 times table is just skip-counting: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30. Say the sequence a few times until it has a beat — most children pick up the rhythm faster than the facts. Once the sequence is there, any single fact can be rebuilt from its neighbor: if 3 × 6 = 18, then 3 × 7 is just one more three, 21.

The unit digits cycle through all ten digits

Look down the answers: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30. The final digits run 3, 6, 9, 2, 5, 8, 1, 4, 7, 0 — every digit exactly once — and then the pattern repeats. That is why 3 × 11 = 33 and 3 × 12 = 36 feel like déjà vu: after 30, the whole sequence starts over, thirty-plus.

The digit check for threes

Any answer in the 3 times table passes a magic test: add its digits and the sum is 3, 6 or 9 (21 → 2 + 1 = 3; 27 → 2 + 7 = 9; 36 → 3 + 6 = 9). Your child can use it to catch mistakes — and it doubles as the divisibility rule for 3 later on.

Build a three out of a two

When the rhythm fails, fall back on addition: 3 × n is (2 × n) + n. Four threes? A double (8) plus one more 4 — 12. It is slower, but it always works, and it turns the table into something your child can reason out instead of freeze on.

3 times table FAQ

What is the easiest way to learn the 3 times table?

Say the skip-counting sequence — 3, 6, 9, 12… — until it has a rhythm, then rebuild single facts from their neighbors: 3 × 7 is 3 × 6 plus one more 3. If that fails, compute (2 × n) + n: a double plus one more group.

How can I check if an answer is really a multiple of 3?

Add the digits of the answer. If the digit sum is 3, 6 or 9 (keep adding if it is a bigger number), the answer is in the 3 times table: 51 → 5 + 1 = 6, so 51 = 3 × 17.

How long does it take to learn the 3 times table?

With a few minutes of counting practice a day, most children have the threes in about a week. Our preset practice round keeps the daily rounds short — five to ten questions is enough.
