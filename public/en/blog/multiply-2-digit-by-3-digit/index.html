How to Multiply 2-Digit Numbers by 3-Digit Numbers

No new skill required — just place value, two partial products, and one addition.

October 26, 2026

Multiplying a 2-digit number by a 3-digit number may look tricky, but you do not need a brand-new math skill. You use the multiplication facts you already know, pay attention to place value, and add two smaller products.

The basic method is simple: multiply by the ones, multiply by the tens, then add.

In this guide, we will work through examples step by step, explain why the method works, and finish with practice problems you can try yourself.

What Does 2-Digit by 3-Digit Multiplication Mean?

A 2-digit number has a tens digit and a ones digit, such as 24, 36, or 57.

A 3-digit number has a hundreds digit, a tens digit, and a ones digit, such as 125, 326, or 408.

Here is an example:

24 × 326

You can also write it this way:

326 × 24

The answer stays the same when you switch the order. For long multiplication, putting the 3-digit number on top usually makes the work easier to organize.

Start with Place Value

The number 24 means 2 tens and 4 ones:

24 = 20 + 4

So multiplying 326 by 24 means multiplying 326 by 20 and by 4, then adding the results:

326 × 24 = (326 × 20) + (326 × 4)

These two smaller answers are called partial products.

This is the idea behind long multiplication. You are not just following a rule—you are breaking one multiplication problem into easier parts.

Step-by-Step Example: 326 × 24

Let’s solve:

326 × 24

Step 1: Multiply by the ones digit

The ones digit of 24 is 4, so first calculate:

326 × 4

Work from right to left:

  • Ones: 4 times 6 is 24. Write 4 in the ones column and carry 2 tens.
  • Tens: 4 times 2 tens is 8 tens. Add the 2 carried tens to get 10 tens. Write 0 in the tens column and carry 1 hundred.
  • Hundreds: 4 times 3 hundreds is 12 hundreds. Add the carried hundred to get 13 hundreds.

Your first partial product is:

326 × 4 = 1,304

Step 2: Multiply by the tens digit

The 2 in 24 represents 20, not 2.

First multiply 326 by 2:

326 × 2 = 652

Then multiply that result by 10:

326 × 20 = 6,520

Your second partial product is 6,520.

In a whole-number multiplication problem, writing a zero in the ones column helps you show that this row represents multiplication by tens.

Step 3: Add the partial products

Now add the two rows:

1,304 + 6,520 = 7,824

So the final answer is:

Answer326 × 24 = 7,824

Keep the ones, tens, hundreds, and thousands columns lined up when you add.

Another Example: 247 × 36

Try the same method with:

247 × 36

First, multiply by the ones digit:

247 × 6 = 1,482

Next, multiply by the tens value:

247 × 30 = 7,410

Finally, add:

1,482 + 7,410 = 8,892

Therefore:

Answer247 × 36 = 8,892

Notice that the second row represents multiplication by 30, not just 3.

What If the 3-Digit Number Contains a Zero?

A zero does not change the method. You still multiply each digit and keep every place-value column in the correct position.

Let’s calculate:

408 × 25

Multiply by 5:

408 × 5 = 2,040

Multiply by 20:

408 × 20 = 8,160

Add the partial products:

2,040 + 8,160 = 10,200

So:

Answer408 × 25 = 10,200

Do not skip a column just because one of the digits is zero. Also remember that a carried amount still needs to be added, even when the digit you are multiplying is zero.

An Alternative: Break Apart the 3-Digit Number

Long multiplication is not the only way to solve these problems. You can also split the 3-digit number into hundreds, tens, and ones.

For example:

326 = 300 + 20 + 6

Then:

24 × 326 = (24 × 300) + (24 × 20) + (24 × 6)

Calculate each part:

24 × 300 = 7,200
24 × 20 = 480
24 × 6 = 144

Add:

7,200 + 480 + 144 = 7,824

You get the same answer.

This expanded method is useful for understanding place value. Once it makes sense, the two-row long multiplication method is a compact way to record the work.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Treating the tens digit like a ones digit

In 24, the 2 means 20.

If your second partial product is 652 instead of 6,520, you have multiplied by 2 rather than 20.

Helpful habit: Say “multiply by twenty” instead of “multiply by two” when you begin the tens row.

2. Forgetting a carried amount

When a digit multiplication gives a result of 10 or more, regroup it into the next column.

Helpful habit: Write carried amounts clearly above the column where you will use them. Clear or cross them out before starting the next partial product.

3. Misaligning the addition

Even correct partial products can lead to a wrong answer if the columns do not line up.

Helpful habit: Use squared paper, or draw light place-value columns.

4. Rushing before the method makes sense

Speed is not the first goal. Accurate multiplication comes from understanding each step.

Helpful habit: Start with untimed practice and explain what each row means.

How to Check Your Answer

An estimate helps you catch answers that are much too large or too small.

For our first example, round 326 to 300 and 24 to 25:

300 × 25 = 7,500

The exact answer, 7,824, is close to 7,500, so it is reasonable.

An estimate is a check, not proof. If the answer looks reasonable but you are still unsure, recalculate the partial products or use the expanded method to check your work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to put the 3-digit number on top?

No. Switching the order of the factors does not change the product. However, putting the 3-digit number on top and the 2-digit number underneath usually makes long multiplication simpler because you only need two partial-product rows.

Why does the second row end in zero?

The second row represents multiplication by a multiple of ten, such as 20 or 30. For whole numbers, that product has zero ones. The zero keeps the place values aligned.

Can a 2-digit number times a 3-digit number have a 5-digit answer?

Yes. For example:

408 × 25 = 10,200

Do not assume the answer must have the same number of digits as either starting number.

Multiply by the ones. Multiply by the tens. Add the results.
